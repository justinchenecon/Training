Pad's top edge at sternal notch. Neutral grip. Pre-load before unracking — retract and depress scapulae so handles move 1–2cm; that's your start.

Zone 2 CardioCONDITIONING
1 block· 20–30 min· 2×/week
1

Why it's here: four days of heavy resistance work with no aerobic base is an incomplete program. This is the counterweight — low intensity, high return.

Pace: conversational. You should be able to speak a full sentence without gasping. Roughly 60–70% of max heart rate. If you're grinding, it's too hard — intensity is not the point here.

Modes: upright bike, incline treadmill walk, or elliptical. 20–30 minutes, twice a week. Doesn't have to be attached to this session — an off day works just as well and keeps your lifting sessions short.

Logging: tap the circle and enter machine, minutes and zone.

  • Cardiovascular — the primary target
  • Aerobic base — supports work capacity between lifting days
  • Aids recovery between lifting days
  • Zero added fatigue at this intensity
  • Turning it into intervals — hard efforts bring back breath-holding
  • Rowing erg — forward lean and hard pulls, defeats the purpose
  • Head-below-heart positions at any point
  • Skipping it because the lifting session ran long — move it to an off day instead

Warm-Up · 9 min

  • Bike easy · 3 min
  • World's greatest stretch · 4/side
  • Standing thoracic extension · 8
  • Band pull-apart · 15
  • Wall slides + wall angels · 10 each

Breathing Protocol

  • Exhale forcefully through every concentric — never hold through a set
  • No belt cinched tight
  • Compounds RIR 1–2 · everything else RIR 2 — never grind
  • Head neutral or above heart
  • Pulldown: stop the set if you start leaning back
01
DeadliftCONVENTIONAL
4 sets· 6 reps· 3 min
1
2
3
4

Breathing note: conventional generates more intra-abdominal pressure than most machine work. Breathing discipline matters more here, not less. Keep loads submaximal — this slot is not for grinding singles.

Feet hip-width, bar over mid-foot, shins ~2cm from bar. Hinge hips back, shins come forward to touch bar. Grip just outside knees. Pull slack out of the bar — pre-tension lats, chest up, ribs down.

Half-breath into the belly — not a maximal breath-hold. Eyes on a point ~2m forward, neutral neck. Drive: push the floor away through mid-foot, hips and shoulders rise at the same rate. Exhale forcefully as the bar passes the knees, finishing the breath at lockout. Reset the breath between every rep — no held-breath multi-rep sets.

  • Glutes + hamstrings primary
  • Erectors isometric (spinal position, not movement)
  • Lats isometric — keep bar tight to body
  • Quads assist off the floor only
  • Holding breath through a full set. Reset between reps
  • Grinding a maximal rep — stop the set instead
  • Hips rising faster than shoulders — turns it into a stiff-leg
  • Head dropping toward the floor, or craning up at the ceiling — stay neutral
  • Bar drifting away from shins — costs the low back
  • Belt cinched tight — keep it loose enough to breathe normally
  • Bouncing reps off the floor — reset each rep
02
Belt SquatSPINE-FREE
4 sets· 6–8 reps· 2:30
1
2
3
4

Best squat option here. Load hangs from your waist via belt — zero spinal compression, no axial load, no need to brace abdomen hard. Replaces traditional barbell squatting safely.

Strap belt around hips (sit on hip bones, not waist). Stand on platform, feet shoulder-width, toes ~15° out. Hold the upper rails for balance only — don't pull yourself up with arms. Stand fully tall to start.

Descend in 2 sec — hips travel back and down, knees track over toes. Go to ~parallel or to where you maintain neutral spine (no lumbar rounding). Drive up through mid-foot. Exhale forcefully through the drive. Stand tall, squeeze glutes, no hyperextension.

Without spinal load you can train this harder than a back squat. Push to RIR 2.

  • Quads + glutes primary — hip-dominant pattern with belt at hips
  • Adductors, hamstrings as stabilizers
  • Core minimal — no axial load to brace against
  • Pulling up on rails with arms — defeats the loading
  • Belt riding up to waist — should sit on hip bones for proper hip extension load
  • Knee valgus — track over second toe
  • Lumbar rounding at depth — stop ROM where neutral spine is lost
  • Hyperextending at top — squeeze glutes to terminate, don't lean back

3 sets· 8–10 / leg· 2 min
1

3

Rear foot on bench, laces down. Bench at knee height. Stride: short = quad bias, long = pure glute. Moderate stride for 50/50. Front shin ~70–80° from horizontal at bottom.

DBs at sides. Torso ~10° forward lean — loads glute through hip flexion. Drop straight down. Drive through mid-foot to heel of front leg. Rear leg = kickstand only.

  • Rear-leg passive hip flexor stretch

  • Front knee caving in — track over second toe
  • Standing torso fully upright — removes glute
04
Iso-Lateral Kneeling Leg Curl
3 sets· 10–12 / leg· 1:30
1
2
3

Kneel on the platform, support leg pad against the working thigh. Working leg's knee aligned with machine pivot, ankle pad just above heel on Achilles. Brace torso against chest pad — hips stay neutral, don't let them flex forward.

The kneeling position extends the hip while flexing the knee — this lengthens the hamstring across both joints, especially the long head of biceps femoris. That makes this a superior stretch position vs. seated.

Toes dorsiflexed throughout (pulled toward shins). Curl heel under butt, 1-sec peak hold. 3-sec eccentric, never let stack rest. Train weaker leg first, match reps on stronger.

  • Hamstrings — biceps femoris (long head) heavily biased due to hip extension
  • Stronger stretch stimulus than seated curl
  • Iso-lateral exposes/fixes left-right strength asymmetry
  • Hips flexing forward — shortens hamstring, kills stretch advantage
  • Plantar-flexing toes — instant deactivation
  • Letting stack rest at bottom — kills tension
  • Using torso to pull weight up — only knee should flex

Single-Leg Calf Raise on Leg Press
4 sets· 10–12 / leg· 1 min
1
2
3
4

On lying leg press. Place ball of one foot on bottom edge of platform — heel free to drop below platform edge. Other leg crosses behind or rests aside. Knee nearly straight (5° bend, locked) — this biases gastrocnemius. Fully locked overstretches, fully bent removes gastroc.

Drop heel toward floor for 2-sec pause at full stretch — this is where gastroc grows. Drive up onto big toe to full plantar flexion, 1-sec squeeze at top. Slow tempo. The 2-sec pause kills the stretch-shortening cycle so muscle does the work, not tendon.

Unilateral = better stimulus per leg, exposes/fixes side-to-side imbalances.

  • Gastrocnemius (medial + lateral heads)
  • Knee mostly straight = gastroc focus
  • Single-leg loading exposes weak-side asymmetry
  • Bouncing — calves are 70% tendon, bouncing skips muscle work
  • Knee bending under load — shifts work to soleus
  • Rolling to outer foot — push through big toe
  • Cutting the stretch — deep stretch is the growth signal

01
Hack SquatQUAD ISOLATION
4 sets· 10–12 reps· 3 min
1
2
3
4

Machine-supported quad work with a fixed path — back braced against the pad, so no balance or hip-drive cheating. Note the load sits on your shoulders, so this carries more axial compression than a pendulum or belt squat: keep it in the 10–12 range and off the grinding end.

Shoulder pads on top of shoulders/traps, back flat against the pad the whole set. Feet on platform — low-to-mid placement for full quad emphasis (high placement shortens ROM and turns it into a partial). Toes 15° out.

Unrack. Lower in 2 sec to ~95° knee flexion, or to where the lumbar starts to peel off the pad — whichever comes first. Drive up through mid-foot. Exhale forcefully through the drive — do not hold the breath through the rep. Stop 5cm short of lockout.

Because the torso is supported, most people can load this heavily. That is exactly why the rep target sits at 10–12 rather than lower: the goal here is quad stimulus, not a maximal effort.

  • Quads (vastus lat., intermedius, rectus fem.) — primary target
  • Glutes and adductors assist out of the bottom
  • Core braced isometrically against the pad, minimal spinal movement
  • Holding the breath through the rep — the main thing to avoid on a shoulder-loaded machine
  • Lumbar peel at depth — non-negotiable hard stop
  • Knee valgus — actively press knees out toward toes
  • Heels lifting — ankle mobility limit; elevate 1–2cm or limit depth
  • Foot too high on platform — turns into a partial, kills quad stretch
  • Bouncing out of the bottom — controlled descent instead
  • Chasing a heavy low-rep set here — wrong lift for it

Standing Hip ThrustGLUTE ISO
3 sets· 10–12 reps· 2 min
1
2
3

Best glute-isolation tool you have. Pure hip extension under load — no spinal involvement, no axial compression. Replaces cable pull-through with a more direct stimulus.

Stand on platform, chest pad against your front (sternum/upper abs). Hold rails for stability. Adjust pad height so the lever arm/pad presses against your upper glutes / lower back where the glute insertion is — not on lumbar spine.

Setup: feet shoulder-width, slight knee bend (~15°, locked). Brace against chest pad. Start position: hips slightly flexed forward.

Drive hips forward by squeezing glutes hard — finish with posterior pelvic tilt at top (tuck tailbone under, ribs down). 1-sec peak squeeze. 2-sec eccentric back to start, never let glutes fully relax.

  • Glutes — gluteus maximus primarily, in pure shortened-position contraction
  • Standing position with pad on glutes = direct loading, no leverage loss
  • Hamstrings minimal (different from hip thrust on bench)
  • Pad on lower back/lumbar — should be on glutes, adjust height
  • Hyperextending lumbar at top — posterior pelvic tilt, don't arch back
  • Knees bending through rep — knee angle stays fixed
  • Pushing with arms on rails — they're for balance only
  • Cutting reps short — squeeze glutes hard at top, full lockout

Smith Machine Seated Calf RaiseSOLEUS
4 sets· 12–15 reps· 1 min
1
2
3
4

Why this and not another straight-leg raise: the gastrocnemius crosses the knee, the soleus doesn't. At 90° of knee bend the gastroc goes slack and the soleus does essentially all the work. Day 2's single-leg leg press covers gastroc; this covers soleus.

Flat bench inside the Smith. A 25kg plate or aerobic step in front for elevation. Balls of feet on the plate, heels free to drop below. Knees at 90° — this angle is the entire point.

Bar across the lower thighs, just above the knees, padded with a towel or foam. Unrack. Upper body upright, head neutral, eyes forward.

Drop the heels deep — 2-sec pause at full stretch. Drive to full plantar flexion, 1-sec squeeze at the top. Slow throughout. Soleus is mostly slow-twitch: it wants range and tension, not load.

Load note: your leg press numbers do not transfer. Start light and find it — the bar across your thighs is a completely different loading position.

  • Soleus — nearly all of the work; gastroc is mechanically taken out by the bent knee
  • Soleus is roughly half of total calf mass and sits under the gastroc — developing it adds lower-calf thickness
  • Slow-twitch dominant: responds to reps, time under tension and full range far more than heavy load
  • Smith bar gives a fixed path, so no balance demand
  • Knee angle opening past 90° — gastroc takes over and the point is lost
  • Bouncing — skips the muscle entirely on a slow-twitch fibre type
  • Cutting the bottom stretch short — that is where it grows
  • Going too heavy — soleus responds to volume, not load
  • Bar too far up the thighs — uncomfortable and shifts the mechanics
  • Torso slumping forward, head dropping — stay upright
